How this works

There's no AI model involved anywhere in this tool — no API, no key, no account, no data leaving your browser. Everything below runs as plain JavaScript on your device.

The scoring, in plain terms

  • Requirements list: built automatically from your JD (lines mentioning "must," "required," "proficient," etc. count extra), and fully editable — remove, add, or toggle Required/Preferred.
  • Requirement match score (70% of the final score): the share of your requirement list found in each resume, with Required items weighted twice as heavily as Preferred ones.
  • Overall similarity score (30% of the final score): a text-overlap score (cosine similarity, if you want the technical term) between the whole JD and the whole resume, so resumes that generally cover similar ground still get some credit even for phrasing outside the requirement list.
  • Synonyms: a small built-in dictionary treats common variants as equivalent — "JS"/"JavaScript," "ML"/"Machine Learning," "Excel"/"MS Excel," and similar pairs.

What it can't do

  • It can't tell that "led a 5-person team to ship a product" demonstrates leadership unless "leadership" (or a synonym) is literally present.
  • It can't judge the quality of writing, depth of a project, or credibility of a claim.
  • A resume that lists many keywords in a skills section can outscore a resume that demonstrates the same skills narratively without naming them.
  • It only catches synonyms in its built-in dictionary — an unusual phrasing may simply be missed.

Getting better results

  • Spend a minute reviewing the auto-extracted requirements before running — remove noise, add anything missing, and mark the true must-haves as Required.
  • Treat the top 10 as a starting shortlist to read yourself, not a final decision.
